PPR Boom Bap, 90 BPM, 4/4
From Drum Programming in Every Genre, lesson 1.1 (pauseplayrepeat.com)

Two folders, same notes:
- Swung: the swing from the lesson (Swing MPC 3000 16ths 61) is written into the drums and bass.
  Every even 16th (steps 2, 4, ... 16) is 25 ticks late at 480 ticks per beat, 34.7 ms at 90 BPM.
  Use these in any DAW. Do not put a groove on them; they already swing.
- Straight: the same notes on the grid. Use these in Live with the Swing MPC 3000 16ths 61 groove
  on the clip, as the lesson does.

Drums are on MIDI channel 10, laid out for Live's Boom Bap Kit (Core Library):
kick 36 (C1 in Live), snare and ghost snares 38 (D1), closed hat 42 (F#1), open hat 46 (A#1),
low tom 43 (G1), high tom 45 (A1), crash 49 (C#2). Live calls middle C (note 60) C3.

Files: Drums - Main 2 bars (the core loop), Drums - Verse 8 bars (fill in bar 8), Drums - Hook 8 bars
(crash on 1), Drums - Fill 1 bar, Drums (the whole song's drums), Bass 4 bars, Keys 4 bars,
Keys - End 1 bar, Vibes - Hook 4 bars.

The keys are rootless voicings, and the bass plays the roots. Together they make Dm9, Gm9, Bbmaj9
and A7(b9), one chord per bar. Without the bass, the same keys sound as Fmaj7, Bbmaj7/F, Dm7 and C#dim7.

stuck in a musical rut and need a dose of inspiration to find interesting 
chord progressions and melodies. Now, let's zoom in on the chord_files 
Progressions, guitar edition. Starting with the front side … We can see a bunch of chord names. At the bottom, we've got two knobs 
allowing you to set the Tonality. Flip it over, and on the back side, you'll find chord 
diagrams with corresponding scale tones, arranged in the same order as the front. Currently, we are holding the guitar version, 
but we've got versions for piano and ukulele too. Looking again at the front side, you can see 
the chords organized into three sections: Main Chords, Secondary 
Dominants, and Modal Interchange. The middle section, Main Chords, contains diatonic 
chords that naturally occur within a key, we set. You may start with any chord and create 
progressions freely within this section. The main chord degrees of the 
Key are highlighted in white: Tonic I Subdominant IV Dominant V If you want to add some extra color to the 
diatonic chords, you can jump to the top section, where you will find Secondary Dominant for 
each of the chord from the middle section. You can jump from any of the Main Chords to any of the chords above. Arrows will 
guide you to the resolution. For example: E7 wants to resolve to A minor, C7 to F Major, A7 to D minor, and so on. If we want to add even more 
“color” to our chord progression, we could jump to any chord in the BOTTOM section. You will find here chords from the parallel minor 
key - we call this concept Modal Interchange. To return to the Main Chord section, 
you are once again guided by arrows. We should also explain the chord 
symbols that are used here. Capital M means Major chord, Lowercase m means minor chord, 7 means Dominant 7 chord. Let’s now flip the file to 
see how to read diagrams. Black dots are chords tones. Golden dots are scale tones. Empty dots are open strings. And the bold line is the Barre grip. The strings are sorted from Low E on 
the bottom to the High E on the top. Great! You are now familiar with the chord_files 
and we are ready to check out some examples. Let’s kick off with a simple one 
and use just the chords from the Middle section which are all from the same Key. Let’s select the Key. I like E Major. And here we can see the

progression with a Secondary Dominant chord. Let’s now explore the Modal Interchange. We know the procedure. We will choose a Key - D Major. I will select a couple of chords from the
